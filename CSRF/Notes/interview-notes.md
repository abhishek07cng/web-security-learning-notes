# Interview Notes - CSRF

## What Is CSRF?

Cross-Site Request Forgery is a vulnerability that tricks authenticated users into performing unintended actions.

---

## Why Does CSRF Occur?

Browsers automatically attach:

- Session Cookies
- HTTP Basic Authentication
- Client Certificates

to requests.

---

## What Are The Three Conditions Required?

1. Relevant Action
2. Cookie-Based Authentication
3. No Unpredictable Parameters

---

## What Is A CSRF Token?

A unique, secret, unpredictable value generated by the server.

---

## Why Are CSRF Tokens Effective?

Attackers cannot predict valid token values.

---

## What Is SameSite?

Browser security mechanism controlling when cookies are sent cross-site.

---

## SameSite Modes

### Strict

```text
No cross-site cookies
```

### Lax

```text
Limited cross-site cookies
```

### None

```text
Cookies always sent
```

---

## Common Token Validation Flaws

- Validation depends on method
- Validation depends on token presence
- Token not tied to session
- Token tied to wrong cookie
- Double submit cookie

---

## Difference Between CSRF And XSS

### CSRF

```text
Forces Actions
```

### XSS

```text
Executes JavaScript
```

---

## Best CSRF Defences

- CSRF Tokens
- SameSite Cookies
- Origin Validation
- Referer Validation

---

## Important Interview Point

Having a CSRF token does NOT mean the application is secure.

Validation logic matters.
